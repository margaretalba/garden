#digital-garden #creativity 
I've been obsessed with digital gardens lately.

Like what do you mean I can have a personal space on the internet where I can share my inspirations, hyper fixations, and learnings - and not be beholden to algorithms?? 

Digital gardens are different than blogs and boards because they are meant to be cultivated. Ideas are added upon and grown (like a garden) and unlike blogs, they are presented in a non-linear format.

With this unique structure, developers were amongst the early adopters since they could code this kind of personalized setup. However, because of this, most of the digital gardens I came across were made and curated by engineers/developers. It wasn't until I saw a creative's take on a digital garden that I saw the possibilities of what it could look like for me: a metalsmith and designer.

This is my list of public digital gardens that you can peruse for inspiration if you are a creative, maker, artist, etc. Although, you can totally enjoy these links if you're not any of those too. 

1. [Caro's Digital Garden](https://garden.carolynyoo.com/) by Carolyn Yoo
This was the first digital garden that helped me visualize what mine could look like. I was particularly inspired by the How To section, because God knows the amount of How-tos I consume via YouTube and Substack and then promptly forget what I learned. 

What I love about Caro's is that most of the pages are just a few sentences. If you're intimidated by the idea of writing complete coherent thoughts off the get-go, this shows that you can still have an effective digital garden with just a few notes. That kind of approach made the idea of digital gardening more accessible for me. 

Side note: Carolyn also wrote an amazing article about digital gardens that I recommend everyone to read. 

2. [Digital Garden](https://app.notion.com/p/digital-garden-202fbdf4ad4480538b87d2fcab8a5803) by Harley Claes
A feast for the eyes! Every corner of Harley's Digital Garden is filled with imagery. It's also one of the best digital gardens I found that's hosted on Notion. I love her take on [Curriculums](https://app.notion.com/p/Curriculums-202fbdf4ad4480f8a176c8673f6153cd) and her page on [Writing Resources](https://app.notion.com/p/Writing-Resources-202fbdf4ad448069bad8dc9e1c3caf41).

3. [Mister Chad](https://mister-chad.com/welcome) by by Chad Bennett
A super robust collection from a designer covering topics like 3D Modeling, animation, graphic design and more. I felt like Mister Chad was a mansion and each link was a new room. My favorite pages include [free images for commercial use](https://mister-chad.com/graphic+design+resources/free+images+for+commercial+use) and [books on creativity](https://mister-chad.com/creativity/books+on+creativity/!+books+on+creativity). 

4. [The Garden ](https://maggieappleton.com/)by Maggie Appleton
Maggie is a developer with an eye for design, and it shows! Her garden's design is particularly inspiring with beautiful typography and fun icon elements. The illustrations also bring a personal touch.

I'll add more as I come across them! But how about you? What would you grow in your digital garden?
