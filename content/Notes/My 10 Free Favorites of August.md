I'm a *sucker* for a favorites list (I've been watching Christines monthly favorites videos for almost, what, 8 years now??). But this past summer, I was going through our house to pick out things to sell at our garage sale and I found *piles* of forgotten makeup, clothes, accessories, crafts. All items I remember thinking "this is needed, this is an investment, I'll use this again"

Welcome to Free Favorites - a list of my hyper fixations that require no buying (unless you count a Forever Stamp), include no affiliate links, and hopefully kee. Y

1. **Youtube Videos for Background Listening**
   I feel like all my friends know at this point, but I LOVE a good YouTube essay or commentator. I'm guilty of treating this videos as podcasts while I draw or make something.
   
   This month, I've been listening more to commentators and here are my current favorites
   
   Amanda from Swell Entertainment
   Amanda attends both big-name and niche events, conferences, balls, etc and reviews them. 
   
   Zach from Swiftologist
   Zach reviews songs and albums and generally comments on pop music. Although I don't agree with some of his points, I ***wish*** I could write like how he thinks. He can dissect a song with such precision, sometimes I find myself rewinding just to hear his sentences again (he also speaks faster than any other YouTuber I know)
2. **Digital Gardens**
   I've dug deeeeeep into digital gardens and I've recommended them to every creative I know. I'm in so deep that my digital garden is, frankly, mostly about digital gardens. 
   
   What's great about them is you don't really need to spend money to create one. Obsidian, Notion, and Are.na are free! I use Obsidian to create my digital garden and use Quartz (also free!) to publish it.
3. **[Public Domain Archive](https://pdimagearchive.org/) and [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/)**
   Free databases for images, books, music, and more. It's kind of like a mini library/museum that you can access through your laptop. I've actually been grabbing screenshots from things I've found on these databases and including them in my digital garden.
4. [Free Toddler Pants Pattern](https://oldmadenew.org/files/2015/04/12-18m_baby_pants_pattern.pdf) by Old Made New
   I feel like every morning, I'm *always* looking for pants for my daughter. Doesn't matter if we just did laundry - there must be a pants gnome in the house. I found this free pattern for pants and made a pair out of a thrifted men's shirts(best place to find patterns! TRUST) in just one afternoon. 
5. [Free Thursdays in Portland](https://portlandartmuseum.org/discounts/)
   Every first Thursday, the Portland Art Museum is free! I used to go to these when I was in my 20s, but I totally forgot about them as I've gotten older. I met up with my friend and brought my daughter since my husband was still working. I braced myself for a chaotic toddler, running away from me anytime she could. But she actually loved seeing all the artwork, and I loved showing them to her.
   
   I also saw permanent exhibits I've never seen before? Like there was an entire room dedicated to paintings of Mt. Hood! Absolutely beautiful.
   
   I know in other cities (Kansas City and Washington D.C. to name two!) art museums are already free. But maybe there are some other "secret" free activities that you might have forgotten about? 
6. [Keep Going](https://austinkleon.com/keepgoing/) by Austin Kleon
   Borrowed this from the library after a loooong stint of DNFs. To the point where I questioned my ability to actually finish a book. I've read Kleon's work in the past and they're easy to read, to the point, and each one inspires me to actually do the work. This book was no exception. I logged my favorite quotes in my digital garden. And YES! I did finish this book. 
7. [Quietly Public](https://cyoo.substack.com/t/quietly-public) series by Carolyn Yoo
   When I deactivated my Instagram in January, I honestly did not know what my future as a creative would look like. Sure, I work full-time, but what if I wanted to release a new collection? How do I share things I'm working on? What would that look like without marketing on Instagram? 
   
   Turns out, Carolyn Yoo has been exploring that herself by interviewing people who are creating their own worlds on the internet. It's also her article on [Digital Gardening](https://cyoo.substack.com/p/intro-to-digital-gardening-a-quietly) that gave me the final (and biggest!) push to start my own. 
8. Olivia Rodrigo and Kacey Musgraves' new albums
   Yes, these two albums released earlier this year, not actually in August. Yes, I'm still including them in this list.
   
   Olivia Rodrigo's You Seem Pretty Sad for a Girl So in Love is an absolutely stunning album. I'm honestly in awe of her songwriting and I wish I had this album to listen to on repeat when I was in my 20s. It would've been hella cathartic. Favorites include: Wrong with Me and Expectations.
   
   Kacey Musgraves
   I first came across Kacey's new music during her Coachella Livestream. I heard Uncertain, Tx, and it was love at first listen. As someone born and raised in Texas and now living in Oregon - it hit this sweet balance of nostalgia and witty songwriting. I've had the entire album on repeat since. Favorites include: Rhinestoned and Dry Spell.
9. Writing letters
   I might say this every single month tbh.  This has been one of my favorite things the entire year. I've been writing so many more letters, I've actually gotten a little craft box ***dedicated*** to letters. I've got vintage stamps, wax seals, postcards, different types stationery and pens. 
   
   It's been my favorite way to catch up with friends. Maybe it's the act of actually putting pen to paper, but I've found the most meaningful conversations (besides talking in person ofc) happen in letters. And I'm near giddy whenever I receive one back in the mail.
